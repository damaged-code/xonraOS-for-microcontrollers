OLED Breadboard Wiring

Everything the clock uses, on one breadboard: the screen, two push buttons, a buzzer and a potentiometer (the knob). The knob is wired for later: the software does not read it yet.

Top view, USB port pointing right. Hover or tap a row in the table to highlight its wire. The board's D-pin side faces the top, so row a above it is free for wires. Buttons straddle the centre gap on the right, the buzzer sits above them, and the potentiometer is below the breadboard with three wires up to row j.

Steps

  1. Unplug the USB cable.
  2. Press the ESP8266 across the centre gap, USB end to the right, with its pins in columns 12–26. Its D-pin side goes in row b, the other side in row i.
  3. Push the OLED's four pins into row e, columns 4–7, reading VCC, GND, SCL, SDA from left to right.
  4. Press the two buttons across the gap: the main one at columns 28 and 30, the second at columns 32 and 34.
  5. Push the buzzer's legs into d29 (the + leg) and d31.
  6. Add the wires from the table, then plug the USB back in.

Before you power it

  • Use male-to-male jumpers. The female-to-female ones won't go into the holes.
  • Each column's 5 holes (a–e or f–j) are connected. Nothing connects across the centre gap.
  • Your board's numbers may not match this picture exactly. What matters is that each wire shares a column with the right pin.
  • Everything here runs from 3V3. Never connect the knob's outer pins to 5 V: A0 only takes up to 3.3 V.
  • If the screen stays blank, check first that SCL goes to D1 and SDA to D2.

The two buttons

  • Wire diagonally opposite legs. It works whichever way round the button is turned.
  • Each pin uses a built-in pull-up: it reads HIGH when the button is up and LOW when pressed. No resistor is needed.
  • Main button (D7): tap = next, hold = select, hold 1.5 s = Home.
  • Second button (D6): its job is set in Settings → D6 button (Off, Home or Back).
  • Avoid D3, D4 and D8 for buttons: the board checks them while starting up.

The buzzer

  • Active buzzers have a sealed black bottom and beep on their own when powered. Passive ones show a green circuit board underneath and need the board to send a tone. Either one works.
  • D8 is checked at start-up and must be low. A buzzer to GND keeps it low, so that is fine.
  • Quick test: Settings → Sound. Turning it on plays a short beep.
  • No sound? The legs are probably the wrong way round. Swap them.

The potentiometer (knob)

  • The two outer pins are the ends of a resistor. The middle pin slides along it as you turn the shaft.
  • With 3V3 on one end and GND on the other, the middle pin gives 0 to 3.3 V. A0 reads that as a number from 0 to 1023.
  • A0 is the board's only analog pin, so one knob is all it can read directly.
  • Swapping the outer wires just turns the knob the other way round.

Not used yet. The clock's software does not read A0 so far. Wiring it now does no harm, and it is ready for a brightness knob or a game dial.
